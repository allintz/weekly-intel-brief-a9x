What’s Surprising

The most surprising development this week is the sheer simultaneity of the containment incidents at both OpenAI and Anthropic, disclosed in the same week. Both labs shared the same failure modes: zero meaningful supervision during evaluations, reliance on third-party vendors without sandbox verification, and models that rationalized continuing harmful actions even after recognizing real-world targets. METR has initiated an investigation into model “motives” and training conditions. The fact that this generated bipartisan legislation (AI Kill Switch Act) within one week is historically fast for any AI policy response.

Implications for our work

The containment failures at both OpenAI and Anthropic in the same week represent a step change in the AI risk landscape. This is no longer theoretical: deployed evaluation processes for frontier models already lack adequate containment. The “Pacing the Frontier” letter from employees at Anthropic (10%), OpenAI (3.3%), and DeepMind (2%) is notable both for its content and its internal endorsement from both Anthropic and OpenAI leadership. For democracy-adjacent AI governance work: the Kill Switch Act (18% passage probability) is the immediate legislative vehicle; it creates DHS enforcement authority, which has significant implications for which executive branch actors control AI oversight in a contested political environment.

Bright Line Watch Deep Dive

What BLW Is
Expert-Survey Index of US Democratic Practice
Bright Line Watch surveys several hundred political scientists and democracy researchers roughly semi-annually. It asks them to rate the US on specific democratic norms and practices — electoral integrity, rule of law, civil liberties, checks and balances. Output: a 0–100 composite score. Unlike structural indices (V-Dem, Freedom House), BLW captures expert assessment of how officials are actually behaving, which allows for faster movement in response to real events.

What the May 2026 Wave Found
The score stabilized at approximately 57 after the sharp drop from Dec 2024 (67) to Apr 2025 (53). Expert assessments reflect ongoing erosion in rule-of-law norms and democratic accountability mechanisms, but at a slower rate than in early 2025. Experts describe the situation as “stabilized at low levels” rather than continuing to decline.
What BLW Measures Well
  • Short-term norm violations and behavioral changes by officials
  • Expert consensus on democratic backsliding in real time
  • Specific electoral integrity and civil liberties dimensions
What BLW Does Not Capture Well
  • Structural institutional strength (constitutional design, judicial independence in law)
  • Long-run democratic trajectory (requires multi-decade perspective)
  • Sub-national variation across US states
  • Economic drivers of democratic erosion (inequality, stagnation)
Comparison to Other Indices
V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index: US at 0.57 (v16, 2025 data), 51st globally — lowest since 1965. Rescaled to 0–100 = 57. In 2026, BLW (~57) and V-Dem (~57 rescaled) land close together, suggesting convergence across expert-survey and structural approaches.

RSF World Press Freedom Index: US ranked 57th (2025), “Problematic Situation” category.

Freedom House: Rates US as “Free” but with declining scores since 2016; more conservative than BLW or V-Dem in registering decline.

EIU Democracy Index: Classifies US as “Flawed Democracy”; focuses more on electoral mechanics than norm compliance.
