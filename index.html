So what

The headline moves this period are on the sweep probability (+3 points to 51.5%) and Vance crossing 50% in the GOP 2028 market; both directional but modest. The Missouri redistricting ruling is the more actionable item: it converts a likely Republican hold into a competitive race with eight weeks to go, and whether SCOTUS grants an emergency stay in the next two to three weeks will determine which map November runs on. Redistricting litigation capacity in state-court systems is proving faster than federal Section 2 challenges at actually affecting November ballots.

The New Hampshire Senate seat is the other immediate variable: the September 8 primary set nominees, and the market (D 81.1%) should sharpen once named candidates enter. New Hampshire is currently the largest Democratic pickup probability on the board, and naming the candidate removes the biggest uncertainty in that race.

So what for our work

The two resolved SCOTUS markets point opposite directions. The birthright citizenship ruling (Yes: SCOTUS struck the EO) is a constitutional check on executive authority over citizenship. Trump v. Slaughter (Yes: upheld firing authority) moves the opposite way, consolidating presidential control over independent regulatory agencies. Both are now settled law, which means the democracy-relevant action has shifted to the cases SCOTUS takes next term and to the administrative states those rulings are already restructuring.

The Missouri redistricting ruling is the most immediately actionable item. State courts moving faster than federal courts on redistricting is now a demonstrated pattern; litigation and advocacy capacity aimed at state constitutional and state-court systems is better positioned than federal Section 2 challenges for the November window. The SCOTUS stay request outcome in the next two to three weeks is the lever.

The 2020 fraud ruling market ticked up 3 points to 11.5%; still thin volume, but the movement is worth watching as a pre-election certification-fight signal. At 11.5% on low volume, it is a precursor indicator rather than a forecast.

Appendix

Bright Line Watch deep dive

What Bright Line Watch is

A recurring survey project run by political scientists at Dartmouth, the University of Chicago, and the University of Rochester. Each wave asks a large panel of political science experts, alongside a representative public sample, to rate how well the United States is currently meeting a list of specific democratic principles and to rate overall democratic performance. Wave 29 surveyed 568 experts and 2,750 members of the public.

The 0–100 scale

Respondents rate US democratic performance from 0 (not a democracy) to 100 (a perfect democracy). The number is an expert-perception measure of democratic practice, not a structural index of institutions or formal rights. It moves faster than structural measures because officials’ behavior can change in months while institutions change over years.

What the July 2026 wave found

Experts perceive a partial rebound from the second-term low points while reporting persistent serious concerns. Specific threats named: partisan mid-decade redistricting, renewed claims of widespread election fraud, and consequential Supreme Court rulings on presidential power and voting rights. Principle-level ratings diverge sharply: 81% of experts judged that parties are not barred by ideology and that elections are free of fraud and manipulation, but only 4% judged that district boundaries do not systematically advantage a party. Expert confidence in accurate vote counting is 94% at state level and 82% nationwide. Republican belief that elections are free from pervasive fraud fell to 25% from 50% in February 2025. Public approval of mid-decade redistricting fell from 30% to 20%, driven by Republican support falling from 52% to 34%. Republican support for hardball tactics dropped by double digits across court expansion, blocking nominees, DC and Puerto Rico statehood, and using DOJ against opponents.

What BLW measures well

  • Short-term norm violations and behavioral changes by officials
  • Expert consensus on democratic backsliding in close to real time
  • Specific electoral integrity and civil liberties dimensions, principle by principle

What BLW does not capture well

  • Structural institutional strength (constitutional design, judicial independence as a matter of law)
  • Long-run democratic trajectory, which needs a multi-decade frame
  • Sub-national variation across US states
  • Economic drivers of democratic erosion such as inequality and stagnation

Comparison to other indices

V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index: US at 0.57 (v16, 2025 data), 51st globally, the lowest since 1965. Rescaled to 0–100 that is 57. With BLW now at 60, the two sit close together; there is no expert-survey versus structural divergence to read into the 2026 numbers.

RSF World Press Freedom Index: US ranked 57th (2025), “Problematic Situation” category.

Freedom House: Rates the US as “Free” with declining scores since 2016; more conservative than BLW or V-Dem in registering decline.

EIU Democracy Index: Classifies the US as a “Flawed Democracy”; weights electoral mechanics more heavily than norm compliance.
