“What’s Surprising” Analysis

The Goodheart Labs combined forecast of 2031 (80% CI: 2027–2044) is notable for how wide the confidence interval is. A 17-year range (2027–2044) reflects genuine expert disagreement rather than just uncertainty about timing — the community cannot agree on whether transformative AI arrives before 2030 or after 2040. OpenAI self-declaring AGI at 12.5% before 2027 remains priced, but the gap between self-declaration and benchmark-passing remains enormous. The Anthropic pause debate on LW this week illustrates the real strategic bind: the lab most concerned about safety cannot exit the race without handing the lead to labs less concerned, which most safety-oriented forecasters consider worse.

Implications for Our Work

  • The 2027–2044 Goodheart confidence interval means planning for AI governance needs to be robust to both fast (pre-2030) and slow (post-2040) timelines. Organizations with 2-year planning horizons may be underweighting the fast-timeline tail.
  • PauseAI UK’s 63-MP open letter shows that formal political coalition-building around AI governance is achievable in the current environment, though translating signatures to regulatory action remains the gap.
  • The Anthropic pause debate is partly about coordination mechanisms. If no multi-lab pause is achievable, the relevant near-term policy lever is regulatory rather than voluntary.

Bright Line Watch — Deep Dive Appendix

What is Bright Line Watch?
Bright Line Watch is a project run by political scientists at universities including Harvard, Yale, and Rochester that conducts expert surveys on US democratic health. Surveys go to ~1,300–1,500 political scientists per wave, asking them to rate the US on 28 specific democratic principles. The 0–100 score is a rescaled aggregate of those ratings. BLW publishes ~2 waves per year (roughly spring and fall).
Scale Interpretation (0–100)
100 = full democracy meeting all principles. 0 = complete breakdown. US scored ~70 in 2017–2019, dropped to ~55 during 2020–2021, partially recovered to ~60 in 2022–2023, and now registers ~44 in Spring 2026. The score captures expert judgments about actual behavior — norm violations, institutional pressure, election administration — not just legal structure.

What the Spring 2026 Wave Found
The Spring 2026 wave registered approximately 44/100, the lowest in BLW history. Expert respondents flagged particular concern about: executive branch interference with independent agencies, pressure on the judiciary, erosion of civil service protections, and election administration changes. The drop from ~50 in Fall 2025 is among the largest single-wave declines on record.

What BLW Measures Well

  • Specific norm violations by named officials
  • Expert consensus on institutional behavior vs. prior norms
  • Real-time response to discrete events (impeachments, court-packing threats, etc.)

What BLW Does Not Capture Well

  • Variation across states (treats the US as a single unit)
  • Subnational resilience (state AGs, state courts holding)
  • Civil society resistance to backsliding
  • Economic context that affects political stability
