So what

The Senate number crossing 50.4% is not a strong signal on its own: the Senate has been within 2 points of 50% for weeks and EBO aggregates fluctuate. What is directionally meaningful is the combination: Texas tilting Democratic, New Hampshire and Maine solidifying, and the generic ballot holding at +5.8. A +5–6 generic ballot environment historically produces significant seat swings in open-seat cycles.

Missouri is now resolved for November election purposes. The SCOTUS stay locks in the existing map; the litigation is over in practical terms. The one-seat implication (Cleaver’s MO-5 stays competitive rather than becoming an impossible 7–1 district) is baked in. The relevant question now is whether the House majority math works under the existing map, which the chamber control markets put at 52.5% for a Dem sweep.

RFK Jr. at 49% for the GOP 2028 nomination is a market anomaly worth flagging but not overinterpreting. The general election market (separate, $705M volume) does not price him as the general-election frontrunner, and the overround in binary nomination markets means two candidates can each show near-50% without that being incoherent. Watch for volume growth on the RFK Jr. market as a signal of whether this is a sustained view or a speculative spike.

EA Forum

  • Maxwell Love — “What the Pro-Democracy Movement Knows About Quitting in Protest” (September 10, EAF, karma 30)
    • Citing Pinckney & Trilling (2024): noncooperation by insiders succeeded approximately 2x as often as public speaking out (67% vs 33%) in anti-authoritarian movements abroad.
    • Loyalty shifts in institutions are rare (23 out of 140 observed opportunities); when they occurred, quiet outreach to insiders was effective ~39% of the time; public protest did worse than no campaign at all.
    • The author’s framework distinguishes "breaking" (visible, public exit) from "binding" (staying and working from inside), each subdivided into speaking, acting, and standing in the way (noncooperation).

This is worth incorporating into frameworks like Hold the Line and similar projects.

Appendix

Bright Line Watch deep dive

What Bright Line Watch is

A recurring survey project run by political scientists at Dartmouth, the University of Chicago, and the University of Rochester. Each wave asks a large panel of political science experts, alongside a representative public sample, to rate how well the United States is currently meeting a list of specific democratic principles and to rate overall democratic performance. Wave 29 surveyed 568 experts and 2,750 members of the public.

The 0–100 scale

Respondents rate US democratic performance from 0 (not a democracy) to 100 (a perfect democracy). The number is an expert-perception measure of democratic practice, not a structural index of institutions or formal rights. It moves faster than structural measures because officials’ behavior can change in months while institutions change over years.

What the July 2026 wave found

Experts perceive a partial rebound from the second-term low points while reporting persistent serious concerns. Specific threats named: partisan mid-decade redistricting, renewed claims of widespread election fraud, and consequential Supreme Court rulings on presidential power and voting rights. Principle-level ratings diverge sharply: 81% of experts judged that parties are not barred by ideology and that elections are free of fraud and manipulation, but only 4% judged that district boundaries do not systematically advantage a party. Expert confidence in accurate vote counting is 94% at state level and 82% nationwide.

What BLW measures well

  • Short-term norm violations and behavioral changes by officials
  • Expert consensus on democratic backsliding in close to real time
  • Specific electoral integrity and civil liberties dimensions, principle by principle

What BLW does not capture well

  • Structural institutional strength (constitutional design, judicial independence as a matter of law)
  • Long-run democratic trajectory, which needs a multi-decade frame
  • Sub-national variation across US states
  • Economic drivers of democratic erosion such as inequality and stagnation

Comparison to other indices

V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index: US at 0.57 (v16, 2025 data), 51st globally, the lowest since 1965. Rescaled to 0–100 that is 57. With BLW now at 60, the two sit close together; there is no expert-survey versus structural divergence to read into the 2026 numbers.

RSF World Press Freedom Index: US ranked 57th (2025), “Problematic Situation” category.

Freedom House: Rates the US as “Free” with declining scores since 2016; more conservative than BLW or V-Dem in registering decline.

EIU Democracy Index: Classifies the US as a “Flawed Democracy”; weights electoral mechanics more heavily than norm compliance.
